title: Hadamard-type lemma from calculus

Let $h:(-\delta,\delta)\times U\to\mathbb{R}$ be differentiable with $h(0,q)=0$
  for all $q\in U$. Then there exists a differentiable
  $g:(-\delta,\delta)\times U\to\mathbb{R}$ with $h(t,q)=t\,g(t,q)$; in particular
  $g(0,q)=\frac{\partial h(t,q)}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0}$.
  (In do Carmo's $C^\infty$ convention $h$ is smooth, so its partial
  $\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}$ is in particular jointly $C^1$ over the
  finite-dimensional $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$; the machine-checked form takes
  exactly this hypothesis. The explicit remainder
  $g(t,q)=\int_0^1\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(ts,q)\,ds$, the factoring
  $h(t,q)=t\,g(t,q)$, the value $g(0,q)=\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(0,q)$, joint
  continuity, and joint \emph{differentiability} of $g$ are all machine-checked.)
