An open, public effort to build Riemannian geometry into a living, machine-verified
textbook — grounded in do Carmo, owned by everyone. Below is the working list of open
problems. Pick one, close it, and your work becomes part of a foundation anyone can reuse.

The work falls into five kinds. The first two build the textbook, the third keeps it
honest, and the last two are the graph and the platform that hold it all together. Each
bullet is a concrete place to start.

## Formalization — proving it in Lean

Write do Carmo's results as Lean proofs a machine can check, closing the open `sorry`s
from the most upstream gap downward.

- **The exponential differential** — `d(exp_p)_v` for a general direction `v`; only the
  `v = 0` case exists today. The core blocker: everything from the Gauss lemma onward
  depends on it. *(Ch. 5)*
- **Hopf–Rinow, unconditional** — the 4 remaining sorries: a minimizing geodesic from the
  geodesic sphere, and metric completeness via Heine–Borel. *(Ch. 7 §2)*
- **The Gauss lemma** — `dist(exp_p v, p) = ‖v‖`, the elementary route from the symmetry
  lemma (3.4, done) and a geodesic variation. *(Ch. 3 §3, Lemma 3.5)*
- **Jacobi fields and the Jacobi equation** — the variation of geodesics and its second
  order theory. *(Ch. 5)*
- **Second variation of energy and the index form.** *(Ch. 9–10)*
- **Conjugate points and the cut locus.** *(Ch. 13)*
- **Comparison theorems** — Rauch, and Bishop–Gromov volume comparison. *(Ch. 10, Ch. 11)*
- **Curvature** — the tensor's symmetries; sectional, Ricci, and scalar curvature. *(Ch. 4)*
- **Spaces of constant curvature.** *(Ch. 8)*

## Exposition — the natural-language textbook

Make the written layer a faithful, readable textbook: every statement transcribed, every
prose argument complete.

- **Fill the missing statements** — Ch. 11 §2.2, Ch. 13 §2.7 and §2.8 are not yet
  transcribed.
- **Faithful transcription of the remaining chapters**, statement by statement.
- **Proof sketches** for results currently stated without one.
- **Worked examples and exercises** carried over from do Carmo.
- **Notation consistency** across chapters — one symbol, one meaning.
- **Strengthen under-linked statements** — long cards with few links usually hide concepts
  that were never wired in.

## Verification — attack and defense

Keep the two layers honest by pitting them against each other: the formal side exposes
holes in the prose, and human review confirms the formal side really proves what it claims.

- **Attack the prose** — use Lean as a logical oracle to surface unstated hypotheses and
  steps that do not follow.
- **Defend the proofs** — review each Lean proof for correctness, not just that it compiles.
- **Bridge faithfulness** — confirm a `(lean, tex)` link formalizes the *same* statement,
  not a weaker or shifted one.
- **Flag "proven modulo interface"** — interface fields that are really axioms in disguise.
- **Audit the derived numbering** against do Carmo's own.

## Meta-mathematics — understanding the structure

Beneath the theorems sits the question of what it means to *understand* one: its real
antecedents and consequences, the cleanest interface to state it through, and how to
measure all of that. This is mathematics about the mathematics.

- **Better interfaces** — for each object (a connection, a metric, curvature), the cleanest
  API a proof should depend on. The interface *is* the reusable surface; a result proven
  only "modulo interface" is exactly a bet on having chosen it well.
- **Antecedents and consequences** — make every result's true dependencies, and what it
  unlocks, explicit as typed edges rather than folklore.
- **The relation taxonomy** — what *kinds* of mathematical dependency exist: `uses`,
  `implies`, `specializes`, `generalizes`, `dual-to`, `example-of`.
- **Hypergraph metrics** — how to measure structure and understanding: coverage, concept
  density, the length-vs-connectivity heuristic for finding gaps.
- **Multi-ary hyperedges** — a theorem depends on its hypotheses *jointly*; a binary edge
  cannot say that. Build the n-ary layer on top of the binary one.

## Knowledge graph — the Astrolabe layer

Keep the content-addressed store itself clean and trustworthy as the graph grows.

- **The self-iterating loop** — make audit → formalize → bridge → re-audit robust and, where
  possible, automatic.
- **Deduplication and merge** of equivalent cards.
- **A coverage dashboard** — what fraction of do Carmo is machine-checked.
- **Identity and integrity** — stable hashing across re-extraction, referential closure as
  the graph is rewritten.

## Platform — visualization and tools

Build the public surface: make a graph of thousands of results legible, fast, and a pleasure
to explore, contribute to, and reuse.

- **Graph layout and visualization** that stays legible at scale.
- **Reader experience** — navigation, search, and cross-references that do not lose the reader.
- **The public website and onboarding** for new contributors.
- **The extract / register / audit toolchain** that moves results between Lean and the store.
- **CI and fitness functions** — architectural linters that catch drift before it lands.

## How we work together

**Challengers** attack the written mathematics — find the holes the prose hides, the
assumption never stated, the step that does not follow.

**Reviewers** defend the record — read a proof and confirm it really proves what it claims.

**Contributors** close the gaps — write, formalize, design, connect. Everything that lands
is shared, reusable, and built on by the next person.
