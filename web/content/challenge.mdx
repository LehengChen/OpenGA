An open, public effort to build Riemannian geometry into a living, machine-verified
textbook — grounded in do Carmo, owned by everyone. Below is the working list of open
problems. Pick one, close it, and your work becomes part of a foundation anyone can reuse.

## Open problems

Each problem is a gap in the formalized textbook. Solving one means a machine-checked
proof, linked back to the natural-language statement it formalizes.

### The exponential differential

**Open · core blocker.** Formalize the differential of the exponential map,
`d(exp_p)_v`, for a general direction `v` — only the `v = 0` case exists today.
Everything from the Gauss lemma onward depends on it.

*do Carmo, Ch. 5.*

### Hopf–Rinow, unconditional

**Open · 4 sorries.** Discharge the remaining gaps in the Hopf–Rinow theorem: the
existence of a minimizing geodesic (geodesic-sphere + connectedness) and metric
completeness via Heine–Borel.

*do Carmo, Ch. 7 §2.*

### The Gauss lemma

**Open · symmetry lemma done.** Prove `dist(exp_p v, p) = ‖v‖` from the symmetry lemma
(3.4, complete) and a geodesic variation — the elementary route, with no second variation.

*do Carmo, Ch. 3 §3, Lemma 3.5.*

## Ways to contribute

### Mathematics

Prove the theorems so a machine can check them; complete the foundations, one result at a time.

### Review

Keep the mathematics honest — on the written side and the formal side; find the gap, flag
the step that does not follow.

### Design &amp; visualization

Make a body of knowledge with thousands of results legible, beautiful, and a pleasure to explore.

### Tools &amp; infrastructure

Build the systems that let everyone read, contribute, and reuse — the public plumbing of the textbook.

## How we work together

**Challengers** attack the written mathematics — find the holes the prose hides, the
assumption never stated, the step that does not follow.

**Reviewers** defend the record — read a proof and confirm it really proves what it claims.

**Contributors** close the gaps — write, formalize, design, connect. Everything that lands
is shared, reusable, and built on by the next person.
