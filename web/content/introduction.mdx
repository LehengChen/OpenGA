Astrolabe stores mathematics as one content-addressed knowledge graph — a body of
results you can read like a textbook, query like a database, and check like a
program, all at once. It is open: anyone can add a statement, a proof, a clearer
exposition, or a missing link.

## What it is

Today mathematics lives in PDFs and in people's heads. We keep it as a graph
instead. Every definition, theorem, and proof is an immutable, hash-identified
card; a natural-language statement and its machine-checked Lean proof sit side by
side, joined by an explicit link; every dependency is an edge. Stored this way, a
result can be reused, recombined, and checked on its own — by a person or by a
machine.

## The questions behind it

The interesting part is not any single proof but what opens up once mathematics is
a shared, machine-checked object: how it should be stored, how its own structure
can guide its growth, and how people — and increasingly technology — will build it
together. These are the [Open Questions](/docs/challenge) driving the project, and
how the graph itself is stored — atoms, edges, identity, derived numbering — is the
[data model](/docs/data-model).

## Projects

The mathematics grows one project at a time. The first is the **Riemannian Geometry
Challenge** — a public, machine-verified textbook of Riemannian geometry — open from
the home page.

## Get involved

Prove a theorem, transcribe or sharpen an exposition, review a proof, improve the
visualization, or fix a broken link. Anything that lands is shared, reusable, and
built on by the next person.
