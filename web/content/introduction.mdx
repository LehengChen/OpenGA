Astrolabe is an attempt to store mathematics as a single, content-addressed
knowledge graph — something you can read like a textbook, query like a database,
and verify like a program, all at once. It is open: anyone can add a statement, a
proof, a better exposition, or a missing link.

## What this is

A body of mathematics, formal and informal, kept in one graph. Every definition,
lemma, and theorem becomes an immutable, content-addressed card; every
machine-checked proof becomes another; and the two are linked. Nothing lives as a
file or a PDF — the artifacts themselves are the atoms, addressed by their
content, so any piece can be reused, merged, or checked on its own.

Our first project builds Riemannian geometry into such a graph: a public,
machine-verified textbook anyone can learn from and contribute to. But the
questions behind it are general — see [Open Questions](/docs/challenge) — and the
infrastructure is meant to carry any subject.

## How it works

A subject lives in the graph as two halves of the same mathematics:

- a **natural-language layer** — the statements, transcribed faithfully;
- a **formal layer** — the same results, written and checked in Lean.

They are joined by cross-source links, so a formal proof and the prose it
formalizes sit side by side. The formal layer acts as a logical oracle that
exposes gaps in the prose; the prose's structure, in turn, shows where the
formalization is still thin. We close that loop, again and again.
